Short answer

To get a transcript and notes of a Google Meet call without a bot, run MeetMind on the computer you join from. It captures the Meet tab's audio together with your microphone, so it doesn't depend on Meet's own recording, a paid Workspace edition or the organiser's settings. When the call ends, your chosen speech-to-text engine transcribes it and Gemini, Groq or Claude writes the minutes.

Three ways to start recording a Meet call

  1. Let MeetMind notice the call (Windows and Linux). When a browser with a Google Meet tab starts using your microphone, a slim overlay at the top of the screen offers to record. It recognises Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox, Opera, Vivaldi and Arc, and needs no extension. On Linux this needs an X11 session; on Wayland, use one of the other two ways.
  2. Use the Chrome extension. The optional extension puts a record button and live processing status inside the Meet tab.
  3. Press Record in the app or the tray menu, or accept the reminder MeetMind shows when a meeting from your connected calendar starts.

A recording started while a Meet call is detected is saved with the Google Meet platform and, when the tab shows it, the meeting link, so you can find the meeting later.

Why not a Google Meet bot?

Many open-source and commercial note-takers for Google Meet are bots: a server runs a browser that joins the meeting as an extra participant and records what it hears. That works when you aren't in the call, but you have to host the bot (or pay someone who does), the host may have to admit it, and everyone sees it in the participant list.

MeetMind is the other approach. It runs on your computer, needs no server, and adds no participant, at the cost of recording only the calls you are in. If you need recordings of meetings you don't attend, a bot is the right tool; for your own calls, a local recorder is simpler and more private.

MeetMind or Google Meet's own transcripts?

Meet's built-in transcripts, recordings and Gemini "Take notes for me" are available only on some Google Workspace editions, and the organiser or an admin usually controls whether they are on. MeetMind works in any meeting you can hear, including on a personal Gmail account and in meetings other organisations host, and you choose the transcription and AI models.

Ask before you record

Because MeetMind adds nothing to the call, Meet doesn't tell anyone it is running. Let the other participants know you are recording, and check the rules that apply to you: some places require everyone's consent.

Frequently asked questions

Does MeetMind work with a free Gmail account?

Yes. MeetMind records the audio on your computer, so it doesn't use any Meet feature that depends on your Google plan.

Will other participants see MeetMind?

No. Nothing joins the meeting and Meet shows no recording indicator for it, so tell people yourself.

Do I need the Chrome extension?

No. On Windows and Linux the desktop app detects Meet calls on its own, and on any system you can press Record. The extension only adds a button inside the Meet tab.

Does it work on a Mac?

Yes, on macOS 14.2 or later (beta). Meeting detection isn't available on macOS yet, so start with Record, a calendar reminder or the Chrome extension.
